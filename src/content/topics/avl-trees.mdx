## Intuition

A regular BST can degenerate into a linked list (e.g., inserting 1,2,3,4,5 gives a chain). AVL trees prevent this by ensuring the height difference between left and right subtrees is at most 1.

After every insertion, we check balance factors and perform **rotations** to restore balance. This guarantees O(log n) height, which means O(log n) search, insert, and delete.

## Formal Concept

### Balance Factor

`BF(node) = height(left subtree) - height(right subtree)`

Valid AVL: BF ∈ {-1, 0, 1}

### Rotation Cases

**LL (Left-Left):** Inserted in left subtree of left child → Single right rotation

**RR (Right-Right):** Inserted in right subtree of right child → Single left rotation

**LR (Left-Right):** Inserted in right subtree of left child → Left rotation on left child, then right rotation on node

**RL (Right-Left):** Inserted in left subtree of right child → Right rotation on right child, then left rotation on node

### Example: Insert 30, 20, 10

```
Insert 30:    30

Insert 20:    30
             /
            20

Insert 10:    30     BF(30) = 2 → LL rotation
             /           ↓
            20          20
           /           /  \
          10          10   30
```

## Step-by-Step Example

### Insert 50, 30, 70, 20, 40, 10:

1. Insert 50: `50` (balanced)
2. Insert 30: `50 / 30` (balanced, BF=1)
3. Insert 70: `50 / 30, 70` (balanced, BF=0)
4. Insert 20: `50 / 30, 70 / 20` (balanced, BF=1)
5. Insert 40: `50 / 30, 70 / 20, 40` (balanced, BF=0)
6. Insert 10: `50 / 30, 70 / 20, 40 / 10` → BF(30) = 2 → LL rotation at 30

After rotation:
```
        50
       /  \
      20   70
     /  \
    10   30
          \
           40
```

## Common Mistakes

1. **Wrong rotation direction** — LL needs right rotation, RR needs left rotation. Students often mix them up.

2. **Forgetting LR/RL double rotation** — A single rotation doesn't fix LR or RL cases. You need two rotations.

3. **Not updating heights** — After rotation, heights must be recalculated bottom-up.

4. **Confusing AVL with Red-Black** — AVL is more strictly balanced (height ≤ 1.44 log n) but has more rotations. Red-Black has fewer rotations but looser balance.

5. **Thinking BST insert is different** — AVL insert = BST insert + balance. The BST part is identical.

2. What's the minimum number of nodes in an AVL tree of height h? (Hint: it's like Fibonacci.)
